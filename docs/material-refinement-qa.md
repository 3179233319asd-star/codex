## Five visual surfaces

1. Layout and spacing: the original central shadow, crossing disc and lensed upper/lower images remain in place. Homepage controls stay clear of the core. The single-screen layout and dialog-based personal content are unchanged; prior interface evidence is preserved in `docs/one-screen-ui-qa.md`.
2. Typography: native rounded/system fonts, local Noto Sans SC 400 / 500 and the existing Chinese text are unchanged. There are no new labels, sections or decorative typography.
3. Color and exposure: the hot upper band retains its overall silver/ivory light, while neighbouring strands and dark gaps remain distinguishable. A gentler tone shoulder, higher bloom threshold and reduced bloom/softening avoid filling the gaps with uniform white. Cool outer gas remains subordinate to the hot material. The central shadow is not lifted.
4. Imagery and motion: a multi-scale turbulent gas body provides the main luminosity; elongated orbital fields and narrower ridges add individual strands. Near-side haze is bent and interrupted, and bright knots move with the gas. Fine stars are less uniformly distributed; broken dust and distant galaxies are slightly more visible. Everything is generated by code, without photos, video or image textures.
5. Components and edges: the original thin photon rim is preserved. Smaller optical softening retains fine edges without adding a sharp halo. Rounded navigation, reading dialogs and the bottom controls remain intact in the desktop, portrait and landscape captures.

## Findings and corrections

- P1, fixed: the first candidate combined reduced emission, narrower ridges and weaker bloom, making the disc an underlit silver line network. The final version restores a continuous luminous gas body and its warm hot layer while keeping fine strands as details.
- P2, fixed: overly narrow, high-power ridges appeared as dots and a mesh. Frequencies, powers and detail weights were reduced, so the gas body carries the visual mass.
- P2, fixed: the second candidate had broad, regular grooves, especially on phones. A multi-scale turbulent body breaks these into variable-width light, dark gaps and irregular patches. The directional fields still provide long orbital flow.
- P2, fixed: uninterrupted foreground streaks and excessive optical softening obscured depth. The foreground now bends and breaks into patches, and post-processing retains its dark gaps.
- P2, fixed: pausing could freeze the last submitted picture while retaining a later simulation time and pointer position. Adjusting brightness then changed the gas/camera frame. Pause now retains the last submitted time and pointer snapshot, so light changes redraw the same frozen scene; the regression check compares the restored framebuffer exactly.

An independent visual review found no remaining P1/P2 issues at normal viewing size on desktop, phone or landscape. Residual fine-art limits remain: the reference has more intricate tangled threads, different individual dust/star locations and somewhat warmer light. This is a moving procedural interpretation; neither pixel-identical reproduction nor an exact physical simulation is claimed. These residual details are not blockers for this refinement.

The following mean sRGB luma proxy is a supporting image measurement, not a physical luminosity measurement.
